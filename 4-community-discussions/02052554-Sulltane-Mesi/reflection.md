## REFLECTION

Writing this research paper helped me understand the current discussions happening inside the software engineering community about AI and its impact on the future of the profession. My paper focused on how AI is not only a technical tool, but also something that is changing the way developers think about their work, their responsibilities, and their future careers. Through the sources I used, I realized that the topic is not as simple as saying AI will replace software engineers or AI will not replace them. Instead, the discussion is more balanced and complicated because AI creates both opportunities and problems.

One of the main ideas I reflected on while writing this paper was that many software engineers see AI as an amplifier. This means that AI can help good developers work faster and more efficiently, but it can also make poor engineering practices worse. If a developer already understands programming, debugging, system design, and testing, AI can become a useful assistant. However, if someone depends on AI without understanding the code, it can lead to lower-quality software and more technical debt. This connects directly to the point in my paper about AI-generated code needing to be reviewed, tested, and understood before it can be trusted.

Another important part of my paper was the discussion about trust in AI-generated code. I found it interesting that even though many developers use AI tools, they still do not fully trust them. This shows that AI is powerful, but it is not perfect. Code created by AI may look correct at first, but it can still contain logic errors, security problems, or misunderstand the real requirements of a project. Because of this, I learned that human developers still have an important responsibility. They are not only writing code anymore, but also checking, validating, and maintaining code that may have been produced by AI.

The part about junior developers and internships also stood out to me. My paper explains that AI may affect junior developers more than senior developers because many beginner tasks can now be automated. This made me think about how difficult it may become for students and new graduates to gain experience if companies reduce internships or entry-level positions. Junior developers usually learn by doing smaller tasks, fixing bugs, writing tests, and slowly gaining confidence. If AI takes over many of those tasks, then the software engineering community will need to find new ways to train beginners and help them enter the field.

I also reflected on the labor market evidence included in the paper. The data showed that early-career workers and new graduates are facing more pressure, while overall software engineering demand has not completely disappeared. This helped me understand that AI does not affect everyone in the same way. Senior developers may be safer because they have experience with architecture, business requirements, debugging, and long-term maintenance. Junior developers, however, may face more competition because companies may expect them to be productive much faster than before.

Another idea that became clearer to me was the connection between productivity and technical debt. AI can help developers produce code quickly, but producing more code does not always mean producing better software. If companies focus only on speed, they may create systems that are harder to understand, maintain, and improve later. This made me realize that software quality depends not only on how fast code is written, but also on how carefully it is reviewed and maintained. In this way, AI can increase productivity, but it can also create hidden problems if it is used without discipline.

Overall, this research paper helped me see that AI is changing software engineering, but it is not removing the need for software engineers. Instead, it is changing what software engineers need to be good at. Developers may write less code by hand in the future, but they will need stronger skills in problem-solving, reviewing AI output, understanding systems, and making responsible decisions. The biggest lesson I took from this paper is that AI replaces some tasks, but it does not replace responsibility. Human developers are still needed to make sure that software is correct, safe, useful, and maintainable.

Personally, this topic made me think more seriously about my own future in software engineering. It showed me that learning to code is still important, but it is also important to understand how to work with AI without becoming dependent on it. AI can be used as a helpful tool, but it should not replace real learning, practice, or critical thinking. After writing this paper, I believe that the future of software engineering will belong to developers who know how to combine technical knowledge with responsible AI use.
